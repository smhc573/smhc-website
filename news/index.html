Our hospitals · October 2026

Sawubona. I see you.

Colleagues from our two hospitals came together for a day of honest conversation, hard questions and a shared plan. It began with one word.

The day opened with something personal. Dr Vishelle Kamath, Medical Director for our hospitals, spoke about growing up in South Africa and introduced the room to a word from her Zulu heritage: sawubona. It translates simply as “I see you”. Its meaning goes much deeper: recognising someone, listening to them, valuing them and making them feel that they matter.

Colleagues from both hospitals talked openly about what is working, what is not, what needs to improve and where we go next. There were exercises and challenges, plenty of laughter and some difficult conversations too. By the end of the day, teams had agreed a clear plan for building on the progress of recent months.

Hawthorne House · October 2026

A five star kitchen

The kitchen team at Hawthorne House has been awarded a 5 for food hygiene, the highest rating there is, under the Food Standards Agency’s Food Hygiene Rating Scheme.

The kitchen was inspected on Friday 2 October, and the result is a fantastic achievement for everyone who works in it.

A top rating does not happen by chance. It reflects the hard work the team puts in every day: how food is prepared, cooked and stored, how the kitchen is kept, and how every check is recorded. Good food, safely prepared, is part of good care. Thank you to Head Chef Alan and the whole kitchen team for setting the standard.

Maple Leaf House · October 2026

So close to ten

Maple Leaf House now has a carehome.co.uk review score of 9.5 out of 10, based on reviews from residents and their families and friends.

The score is based on 9 reviews from service users and residents’ family and friends, and the maximum is 10. It is fantastic to see such lovely feedback recognising the dedication, care and commitment of the whole team.

Our culture · October 2026

Speak up. Listen up. Follow up.

October is Freedom to Speak Up Month, and speaking up is how we make “I see you” and “I hear you” true for everyone.

Our colleagues know our services best because they live them every day. When someone speaks up about something that does not feel right, shares a concern or suggests an idea, they help us protect people, support colleagues and improve care.

We promise to listen with respect, take every concern seriously and follow up so people know what is happening. Speaking up is not about blame. It is about care.
